## The problem

Brandoris Agency, a performance marketing agency with offices in Germany and the UAE, needed a way to prove and sell a new category of service to clients: how visible a brand actually is inside AI-generated answers — ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot — not just classic Google SEO. The product spec (a full scoring model, questionnaire, and database schema) already existed on paper. Building it was assigned as a solo internship project, with no other developers on staff to share the work.

## Architecture

Next.js 16 (App Router, TypeScript) on Vercel, Supabase (Postgres with Row Level Security) for data and auth, Inngest for background job orchestration, and the Anthropic SDK for the actual live AI test engine. The hardest, most differentiating piece was built first, ahead of the easier UI work: a Claude query pipeline that deliberately splits "ask the real customer-style question" and "extract structured signals from the answer" into two separate calls, so the extraction step can't bias the answer being scored. Every live check fires the same prompt three times in parallel through Inngest — each run independently retryable — then averages the results, trading a single noisy LLM call for a statistically reliable one.

Score history is immutable by construction: a new audit always inserts a new row, nothing is ever overwritten, enforced as a database constraint rather than application discipline, so a brand's visibility trend can't quietly drift. Every AI engine call sits behind a shared adapter interface, so the roadmap's planned ChatGPT and Gemini integrations are a drop-in swap rather than a rewrite of the scoring and dashboard layers later. When the need came up to demo multi-brand tracking to the agency, that feature was pulled forward from a later roadmap phase and every route restructured to be brand-scoped rather than assuming one site per account. A CI pipeline runs the full unit test suite on every push, deliberately kept separate from the real API/database integration checks, which cost real money per run and stay manual.

## Outcome

Nearly the entire MVP roadmap shipped solo: auth, onboarding, the live Claude scoring pipeline, a dashboard, competitor intelligence, keyword-level analysis, a content-gap map, a permanent activity log, and a rule-based priority action roadmap — 26 of 27 planned modules, with only Stripe billing deliberately deferred until there's a paying customer to justify building it. The product is live in production and used for real by the agency's own supervisor on a real account, not a staged demo. Nearly every stage was verified against the actual running system rather than just "it compiles" — that discipline caught real bugs a compiler never would, including a UI race condition in the check button's loading state and a rate limiter that was silently burning a free-tier user's entire weekly quota just from signing up.
